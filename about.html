About Sacmar

A trusted partner in Zimbabwean tobacco

For over a decade we have been committed to delivering the finest quality flue-cured Virginia tobacco to customers across the globe.

Welcome Message

Quality begins at the farm

Welcome to Sacmar Leaf Tobacco. We have built our name on consistent grading, sustainable farming and long grower relationships.

At Sacmar, we understand that quality begins at the farm. Through our contract farming system we work closely with tobacco growers across Zimbabwe's main tobacco-growing regions, providing them with the technical expertise, inputs and support needed to produce leaf that meets international standards. Our commitment extends beyond business. We invest in our farming communities through education, infrastructure development and sustainable agriculture initiatives.

Company History

Since 2009

Sacmar Leaf Tobacco was established in 2009, during a pivotal period in Zimbabwe's tobacco industry, and has grown steadily since.

Our journey began with a commitment to quality and a deep understanding of Zimbabwe's renowned flue-cured Virginia tobacco. From our early days, we recognised that the key to success lay in building strong relationships with tobacco farmers and maintaining the highest standards of leaf quality and processing.

Vision

Where we are going

Two commitments that shape every decision we take, from which farms we contract to which orders we accept.

01

Industry leadership

To be recognised as the leading tobacco leaf merchant in Zimbabwe, setting the benchmark for quality and farmer partnerships.

02

Sustainable growth

To balance commercial success with environmental care, so the business and its growers both last.

Mission

How we get there

01

Quality first

To supply flue-cured Virginia that meets international standards and our customers' blend requirements.

02

Farmer partnership

To back our contracted farmers with agronomy in the field, fair pricing and access to quality inputs.

03

Sustainable practices

To promote afforestation, efficient curing and soil conservation, so Zimbabwe can keep growing the crop.
